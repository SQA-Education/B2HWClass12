1. Reading

Making a Resume Stronger

When people apply for jobs online, an Applicant Tracking System may scan their resumes for keywords. That is why it helps to match your resume language to the job description.

For example, if a posting says “manage schedules” and “create reports,” a strong resume might say, “Managed scheduling for five executives” and “Created weekly reports for team meetings.” Action verbs like managed, organized, developed, supported, and improved make experience sound clearer and stronger.

Good resume bullet points are short, specific, and usually written in the past tense. Adding results or numbers can make them even stronger.
